# Making an institutional G10 forex bot significantly more profitable

**The most impactful gaps in this trading system are not in model architecture but in data inputs, risk controls, and validation methodology.** Three categories of improvements stand out above all others: missing macro signals that institutional desks consider essential (FX options data, real rate differentials, cross-currency basis), critical risk blindspots (unmonitored net currency exposure, static position sizing despite correlation), and validation weaknesses (no purged cross-validation, no triple barrier labeling, unrealistic spread modeling). Implementing the top-priority items below could materially improve risk-adjusted returns — conservatively by **0.3–0.5 Sharpe points** — while the risk management fixes address hidden tail exposures that could otherwise cause catastrophic losses during correlated stress events.

The bot's existing 6-analyst architecture and regime framework are genuinely sophisticated. But several features that institutional FX desks treat as non-negotiable are entirely absent, and the ML pipeline has validation gaps that likely overstate backtested performance. What follows is a prioritized roadmap across all eight research domains, with specific implementation guidance and trading rationale for each item.

## Tier 1: High-impact changes that should be built first

### Missing macro signals institutional desks consider essential

**FX options data (risk reversals, butterfly spreads, IV/RV ratio)** is the single largest data gap. Institutional desks live by these signals; the bot completely lacks them. The 25-delta risk reversal measures the implied volatility skew between OTM calls and puts — a positive RR indicates the market is paying more for upside protection, signaling bullish positioning. When risk reversals diverge from spot price, they predict directional reversals. The 25-delta butterfly spread measures tail-risk pricing and maps directly to the bot's regime system: elevated butterfly values signal imminent large moves, confirming THV or PNS regimes. Most actionably, the **ATM implied vol / realized vol ratio** should feed directly into regime classification — values above 1.2 suggest mean-reversion conditions (options overpricing risk), while values below 0.8 signal trending/breakout regimes. Data is available via Bloomberg or CME options on FX futures.

**Real interest rate differentials** should replace or supplement the existing nominal yield features. Nominal yield spreads can be misleading when inflation diverges between economies: a country offering 5% yields with 4% inflation provides only 1% real return. Real rate differentials — constructed as `(nominal yield − inflation breakeven)` for each country — isolate purchasing-power-adjusted returns, which is what capital actually responds to. Morgan Stanley's 2025 outlook explicitly uses a four-regime USD framework keyed on real rates. Construction for the bot: use TIPS breakeven rates (available via FRED) for US, and inflation swap rates for other G10 economies. Add both 2Y and 10Y real rate differentials, with 5-day and 20-day momentum as features.

**Cross-currency basis swaps** capture dollar funding stress invisible in published rates. A negative EUR/USD basis means USD is scarce — dealers pay a premium to borrow dollars synthetically. Macrosynergy research links a negative dollar basis to aggregate USD strength, rising volatility, and deteriorating FX liquidity. During the 2008 crisis, the EUR/USD 3-month basis reached −300bp. For the bot, tracking 3M basis levels and their z-scores across EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, and USD/CAD provides a real-time gauge of systemic stress and a direct carry cost adjustment. CME launched basis futures in July 2024.

**OIS-implied rate expectations** are among the strongest short-term FX predictors. The gap between market-traded overnight rates (SOFR, €STR, SONIA) and policy rates reveals real funding conditions. More critically, changes in the OIS-implied rate path at 3-month, 6-month, and 1-year horizons for each G10 central bank move currencies intraday. One-week changes in OIS expectations are particularly powerful — they capture the market repricing monetary policy in real time, which is what institutional FX traders actually trade on.

**Commodity-currency correlations** for AUD, NZD, and CAD are well-documented but regime-dependent. The correlations are not static: AUD's correlation with iron ore ranges from +0.6 to +0.8 during normal regimes but can collapse when USD dynamics or domestic policy override commodity impulses. The bot should use rolling 30-day correlations as a regime indicator and only trade commodity-FX signals on confluence of three factors: commodity trend, USD backdrop, and domestic data. Add iron ore, copper, WTI, and GDT dairy prices as direct features for relevant pairs. **China PMI above or below 50** is a critical confirmation filter for AUD commodity signals.

### Risk management fixes that prevent catastrophic losses

**Cross-pair exposure netting** is the single most critical risk gap. With 3 concurrent positions across 12 G10 pairs, the bot can easily stack trades that are all effectively "short USD" — long EURUSD, long GBPUSD, and long AUDUSD simultaneously. A single USD event (FOMC surprise, NFP miss) would trigger all stop-losses at once. The fix: decompose each position into component currency exposures, net across all open positions, and set hard limits — maximum net exposure to any single currency should not exceed **4% of equity**, with total correlated-direction exposure capped at 6%. Every new trade should be checked against these limits before execution.

**Correlation-based position sizing** addresses the fact that two positions at 2% risk each don't create 4% portfolio risk when correlated. EURUSD and GBPUSD at ρ ≈ 0.85 create approximately 3.7% effective risk. Implementation: maintain a 60-day exponentially weighted rolling correlation matrix (λ = 0.94) across all 12 pairs. When opening a new trade while another is live, apply a correlation penalty: `adjusted_risk = base_risk × (1 − |ρ| × 0.5)`. If correlation exceeds 0.75, cap the new trade at 1% risk. If it exceeds 0.90, block the trade entirely. For a more sophisticated approach, DCC-GARCH captures time-varying correlations, but the simple penalty formula captures most of the benefit at a fraction of the complexity.

**Graduated drawdown scaling** should replace the binary −5% kill switch. A tiered system preserves capital more effectively: at 0% to −3% drawdown, maintain full 2% risk with 3 concurrent positions; at −3% to −5%, reduce to 1.5% risk with 2 positions; at −5% to −8%, drop to 1% risk with 1 position; below −10%, halt entirely. Crucially, after a drawdown, don't snap back to full risk — increase by 0.25% per profitable week until restored. The mathematical reality is stark: recovering from a 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain, while recovering from 20% requires only 25%.

**Regime-specific risk budgets** are a natural extension of the existing 4-regime system. The parameters should differ dramatically: TLV (trending low vol) gets full 2% risk, 3 positions, and 0.5× Kelly — this is the regime where the bot's edge is strongest. THV (trending high vol) drops to 1.5% risk and 2 positions because wider stops consume more capital. MR (mean-reverting) drops further to 1% and tighter exits. **PNS (panic/no-signal) should trade zero** — flatten all positions within 5 minutes. During regime transitions, blend risk budgets using the classifier's probability outputs rather than hard-switching.

### ML validation gaps that likely overstate backtested performance

**Triple barrier labeling** should replace whatever labeling method is currently in use. Lopez de Prado's triple barrier method encloses every trade in a box defined by take-profit, stop-loss, and maximum time horizon. For M15 with 30min-4hr holds: set the upper barrier at 1.5–2× rolling ATR(14), lower at 1–1.5× ATR, and vertical at 16 M15 bars (4 hours). The evidence is clear: fixed-time-horizon labels produce classifiers whose ROC curves cling to the diagonal (useless), while triple barrier labels show tangible predictive power. Furthermore, the bot's gate logic (fundamental → technical → sentiment) is a natural primary model for **meta-labeling** — a secondary ML model predicting whether the primary signal will be profitable.

**Purged combinatorial cross-validation (CPCV)** prevents the lookahead bias that standard cross-validation permits in time series. For M15 data with 4-hour maximum holding periods: purge 16 bars (4 hours) around test fold boundaries, and add a 32-bar (8 hours) embargo gap. CPCV with N=6 groups and k=2 test groups yields 15 unique splits and 5 backtest paths, enabling calculation of the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO). A 2024 ScienceDirect study found CPCV achieves lower PBO and superior Deflated Sharpe Ratio compared to walk-forward optimization, which "exhibits notable shortcomings in false discovery prevention." Target PBO below 0.3 for production models.

**Realistic spread and slippage modeling** is essential for honest backtesting. Spreads vary dramatically: EURUSD ranges from **0.1–0.3 pips during London-NY overlap to 2–3× wider during the Asian session**, and can spike 3–10× during NFP or FOMC releases. Build a spread model as a function of pair, hour, session, news proximity, and volatility. Use 90th percentile spreads (not medians) for conservative backtesting. For slippage: assume 0.1–0.5 pips on majors during peak hours, 0.5–2 pips during low liquidity, 0.3–1.0 pips on crosses. Historical tick data with bid-ask is available from Dukascopy (free) and TrueFX.

## Tier 2: High-impact improvements for the next development phase

### Execution timing and session optimization

**Session-pair filtering** is a low-effort, high-reward enhancement. Not all pairs should trade in all sessions. EURGBP during the Asian session has very low volume and wide spreads — the bot should either disable it or reduce position sizes sharply. Conversely, JPY crosses are most liquid during the Tokyo session, and USDCAD comes alive only during the NY session when Canadian data flows in. Implement a session multiplier: Asian-only signals at 0.7×, London at 1.0×, London-NY overlap at 1.2×, post-London NY at 0.8×. The London-NY overlap (13:00–16:00 UTC) accounts for roughly 70% of the daily range in EUR and GBP pairs and offers the tightest spreads.

**Fix avoidance and exploitation** deserves specific logic. The London 4pm WMR fix (15:57:30–16:02:30 London time) concentrates massive institutional rebalancing flows, creating predictable pre-fix momentum followed by post-fix mean reversion. Research from Krohn (2024, Journal of Finance) confirms a robust "W-shaped" USD return pattern across 21 years: USD appreciates into fixes and depreciates after. The bot should avoid entering new trades from 15:30–16:15 London time unless specifically trading the fix pattern. At month-end, rebalancing flows dominate — when US equities outperform, institutional rebalancing creates USD selling at the fix. The Tokyo fix at 9:55 JST creates a transitory USDJPY spike with rapid mean reversion; avoid entries at 00:50–01:05 GMT.

**Month-end and quarter-end rebalancing** flows concentrate in the last 2–3 trading days of each month and peak at the final day's London fix. Build a simple rebalancing model: compare MSCI USA versus MSCI Europe/Japan/UK/Australia monthly performance to predict fix direction. In the final trading days, bias signals in the direction of predicted rebalancing. Quarter-end effects are approximately **3× as powerful** as regular month-end. Japan's fiscal year-end (March 31) further amplifies JPY flows.

### ML architecture improvements with proven payoff

**Regime-conditional ensemble weighting** leverages the existing multi-model stack more effectively. Rather than static weights for PatchTSMixer + TCN + LightGBM, optimize weights per regime: LightGBM may dominate in MR regimes (tree-based models excel at nonlinear mean-reversion patterns), while PatchTSMixer excels in trending THV regimes (transformers capture sequential dependencies). Use the regime classifier's probability outputs as a gating function: `final_pred = Σ P(regime_i) × model_i(features)`. Track inter-model correlation — diversification benefit disappears when correlation exceeds 0.7, which should reduce position sizing via the PPO agent.

**Feature importance drift monitoring** catches model decay before it shows up in P&L. Implement a multi-layer system: weekly PSI (Population Stability Index) on the top 20 features (threshold: PSI > 0.25 triggers investigation), bi-weekly SHAP distribution comparison using KS-tests (not on raw features, but on SHAP values — this identifies only effective shifts), monthly adversarial validation (train a classifier to distinguish recent data from training data; AUC > 0.7 triggers retraining). ADWIN (Adaptive Windowing) can provide streaming detection of prediction quality degradation. Crucially, monitor rolling Sharpe ratio with a CUSUM filter for regime-aware drift alerts.

**Conformal prediction for calibrated uncertainty** transforms point predictions into actionable confidence intervals. Classical conformal prediction assumes exchangeable data, which time series violates. The best methods for non-stationary forex are Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI), which dynamically adjusts target coverage via online learning, and Bellman Conformal Inference (BCI), which explicitly optimizes interval lengths under arbitrary distribution shifts. Implementation: generate point predictions from the ensemble, compute conformity scores using rolling cross-validation residuals (last 500 M15 bars), construct 80% and 95% prediction intervals, and **feed interval width directly into the PPO position sizer's state space**. Wider intervals → smaller positions. This creates genuinely adaptive position sizing based on calibrated model uncertainty.

### Portfolio-level risk measurement

**VaR/CVaR portfolio limits** provide the missing portfolio-level risk view. Historical simulation VaR is preferred for FX due to fat-tailed return distributions (parametric VaR systematically underestimates tail risk). Basel III FRTB now mandates **97.5% Expected Shortfall (CVaR)** rather than VaR — CVaR is a coherent risk measure that captures what happens beyond the VaR threshold. Calculate 4-hour CVaR at both 95% and 99% confidence for the bot's holding period. Set limits: daily portfolio CVaR₉₉% ≤ 3% of equity, intraday 4-hour CVaR₉₅% ≤ 1.5%. If breached, reduce position sizes proportionally or block new trades. Use at least 500 days of M15 returns across all 12 pairs for the historical simulation.

**Correlation breakdown detection** catches the most dangerous market events. During risk-off episodes, historically uncorrelated pairs suddenly move together — all non-USD currencies selling off simultaneously. Monitor the first principal component's explained variance across the 12 pairs: normally PC1 explains roughly 35–40% of variance, but when it exceeds 60%, the market has collapsed into a single "risk-on/risk-off" factor. Practical triggers: when average pairwise |ρ| across active pairs exceeds 0.7, reduce to 50% risk and limit to 1 position. A quick real-time proxy: when DXY 1-hour ATR exceeds 2× its 20-day average, assume correlation breakdown.

## Tier 3: Valuable additions with strong cost-benefit ratios

### Alternative data sources worth the implementation effort

**Retail FX positioning as a contrarian signal** is the easiest high-value alternative data to add — it's free, pre-formatted, and proven. IG Client Sentiment, OANDA Order Book, Myfxbook Community Outlook, and Dukascopy SWFX all provide long/short ratios of retail traders. Since 76% of retail accounts are unprofitable (IG disclosure), extreme positioning works as a contrarian indicator: when retail traders are **>70% one-directional in a trending market**, fading them has persistent alpha. The signal persists for hours to days, fitting the M15/30min–4hr hold period well. Combine multiple broker sources to reduce single-broker noise.

**GDELT news analytics** adds structured event-level data orthogonal to the existing LLaMA/Claude sentiment. GDELT is free, updated every 15 minutes, and covers 100+ languages. A 2025 arXiv paper using GDELT + FinBERT features achieved extraordinary out-of-sample Sharpe ratios (5.87 for EUR/USD) using XGBoost — though these numbers warrant healthy skepticism despite expanding-window validation. The key features are not raw sentiment but **article volume, sentiment dispersion, and Goldstein conflict scores** with rolling statistics per country pair. These event-level structured signals are genuinely different from the bot's general-purpose LLM sentiment.

**Central bank hawkish/dovish NLP scoring** can use the existing Claude Opus infrastructure. BIS Working Paper 1215 showed RoBERTa fine-tuned on central bank text achieves **88% accuracy** classifying sentences as hawkish/dovish/neutral. The critical insight for FX: the *relative shift* between two central banks' scores predicts the cross-rate direction. Score all speeches, minutes, and press conferences for the 8 G10 central banks on a [-2, +2] scale. Pre-trained CB-LM models are available in research code. Signal strength is highest around event windows (speeches, minutes release) and decays over days to weeks.

### Carry trade refinements that compound returns

**The carry-to-risk ratio** is a trivial-to-compute improvement that immediately sharpens position sizing. Defined as the annualized interest rate differential divided by annualized FX implied volatility, this metric normalizes carry attractiveness for risk. Banque de France (2025) research confirms that carry-vol ratios identify superior strategies compared to raw rate differentials alone. Position sizing formula: `size = base × (carry_to_risk / median_carry_to_risk_across_pairs)`, capped at 2× base and floored at 0.25×. This automatically reduces carry exposure when volatility spikes — exactly when carry unwinds happen.

**Regime-conditional carry allocation** addresses the well-documented fact that simple carry has been flat since 2010. The solution: condition carry on both volatility regime and valuation. In TLV (low vol) regimes, carry has historically produced Sharpe ratios of 0.7–1.0 — allocate at 1.5× weight. In PNS (panic), carry returns are strongly negative — allocate zero and exit immediately. Macrosynergy research shows that **valuation-adjusted carry** (scaling the carry signal by REER z-score) continues to produce positive returns with Sharpe 0.53–0.62. High carry plus an undervalued currency is a strong signal; high carry plus an overvalued currency is suspect.

**A composite carry unwind risk score** provides early warning. Combine VIX z-score, FX cross-pair correlation spikes, JPY risk reversal changes, cross-currency basis widening, and COT positioning concentration into a single metric. When the score exceeds a calibrated threshold, reduce carry positions to zero and potentially reverse into safe-haven longs. The August 2024 yen carry unwind — which sent JPY from worst to best G10 performer in weeks — was preceded by every one of these individual signals.

### VPIN and order flow toxicity

**VPIN (Volume-synchronized Probability of Informed Trading)** from Easley, López de Prado, and O'Hara measures whether order flow is information-driven versus noise. The CDF of VPIN at the 0.97 threshold predicted the 2010 Flash Crash more than an hour in advance. For the bot: compute VPIN on M5 volume bars for each G10 pair, using bulk volume classification. High VPIN (>0.7–0.8) signals toxic flow with high adverse selection risk — avoid new entries and widen stops. Low VPIN signals balanced flow with better execution quality. Combine with the bot's existing order flow features for a superior risk filter.

## Tier 4: Lower-priority items and research-stage ideas

### Monte Carlo robustness testing and adversarial validation

Monte Carlo simulation across three dimensions — trade reshuffling (1,000+ random reorderings), parameter perturbation (±5–10%), and execution degradation (5–10% random skipped trades, added slippage) — provides a distribution of outcomes rather than a single equity curve. Require **80%+ of simulations to remain profitable** and report performance at the 90th percentile. Block bootstrap preserves autocorrelation structure. Adversarial validation (training a classifier to distinguish training data from recent production data) should run weekly; if AUC exceeds 0.7, the current model's training distribution has shifted enough to warrant retraining.

### Complexity features and advanced time-series models

Rolling Hurst exponent (252-bar M15 window ≈ 63 hours) is a strong regime indicator: H > 0.5 signals persistence/trending, H < 0.5 signals mean-reversion, H ≈ 0.5 signals a random walk where position sizes should shrink. MDPI (2024) confirms complexity measures significantly enhance deep learning accuracy in forex volatility prediction. Wavelet decomposition into 4–8 frequency bands provides multi-scale analysis with demonstrated 12.3% RMSE reduction. For the transformer stack, **iTransformer** (which treats each variable as a token rather than each time step) is the strongest candidate to complement PatchTSMixer for cross-pair feature interactions.

### Stress testing and Kelly criterion

Historical stress replay — feeding tick data from SNB floor removal (Jan 2015), Brexit (Jun 2016), COVID crash (Mar 2020), yen carry unwind (Aug 2024), and the January 2019 flash crash through the bot's logic — reveals whether kill switches and regime detectors actually fire in time. Reverse stress tests ("what scenario causes a −20% drawdown?") identify unknown vulnerabilities. For Kelly criterion: use quarter Kelly (25% of theoretical optimum) per regime-pair combination — this captures roughly half of maximum geometric growth while reducing drawdowns by 75%. Dynamic estimation using rolling 30–50 trade windows per regime allows the PPO agent to be informed by Kelly bounds. Floor at 0.25%, ceiling at 2.5% regardless of Kelly output.

## Conclusion: what to build and in what order

The highest-value improvements fall into three categories, none of which require new model architectures. **First, add the missing data signals**: FX options (risk reversals, IV/RV ratio), real rate differentials, cross-currency basis, OIS rate expectations, and retail positioning. These are what separate institutional-quality models from retail ones, and most are available through Bloomberg, CME, or free sources like FRED and IG sentiment. **Second, fix the risk blindspots**: cross-pair exposure netting, correlation-based sizing, graduated drawdown scaling, and regime-specific risk budgets. The exposure netting fix alone could prevent the bot's worst potential loss scenario — three simultaneously correlated stop-outs during a single USD event. **Third, harden the validation pipeline**: triple barrier labeling, purged CPCV, realistic spread/slippage modeling, and Monte Carlo robustness testing. These don't generate new alpha but prevent the most common failure mode of algorithmic strategies: overfit backtests that collapse in live trading.

The carry-to-risk ratio, session-pair filtering, and fix avoidance windows are particularly attractive because they require minimal development time but immediately improve both signal quality and execution. The VPIN toxicity filter, conformal prediction for position sizing, and regime-conditional ensemble weighting represent the next tier — more complex to implement but offering genuine architectural advantages. Items like MAML meta-learning, satellite data, and SWIFT flows belong on a research roadmap rather than a production sprint — their theoretical appeal exceeds their demonstrated FX alpha at M15 timeframes.
